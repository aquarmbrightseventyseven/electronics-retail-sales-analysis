---

## 1. Protect and grow the California cluster, but reduce dependency risk

San Francisco and Los Angeles together generate **$13.7M — almost 40% of total revenue** — while Portland, ME contributes just 1.3%. This concentration is both a strength and a vulnerability.

**Recommendation:** Continue investing in California (inventory depth, faster delivery, localized marketing) since it's clearly the strongest market. At the same time, run a focused review of Portland, ME and Austin, TX — the two lowest-revenue cities — to determine whether they are early-stage markets worth investing in or markets to deprioritise.

---

## 2. Scale inventory and staffing hard for Q4 — especially December

December generated **$4.6M**, more than 2.5× January's $1.8M. October was the second-strongest month at $3.7M. This points to a clear holiday-driven demand curve.

**Recommendation:** Build inventory and fulfilment capacity plans around an October–December surge, with December as the peak month. Use January–February (the two weakest months) for clearance promotions and inventory rebalancing rather than treating every month equally.

---

## 3. Time marketing pushes to the two daily peaks: late morning and early evening

Orders peak at **11 AM–1 PM** and again at **6–8 PM**, while 2–5 AM is almost dormant (under $250K combined across those 4 hours, vs. $2.3M+ in the single best hour).

**Recommendation:** Schedule email campaigns, push notifications, and flash sales to land at 11 AM and 6 PM — when customers are demonstrably already engaged — rather than spreading marketing spend evenly across the day.

---

## 4. Rebalance product strategy around two distinct economics: high-ticket vs high-volume

The Macbook Pro Laptop earns **$8M from just 4,728 units** ($1,700/unit), while Apple Airpods Headphones earn **$2.3M from 15,661 units** ($150/unit). These are fundamentally different product economics requiring different strategies.

**Recommendation:** For high-ticket items (Macbook Pro, ThinkPad, iPhone, Google Phone), focus on financing options, trade-in programs, and premium service bundles to protect margin on big-ticket purchases. For high-volume accessories (Airpods, cables), focus on cross-sell at checkout — bundling them with laptop/phone purchases captures incremental revenue at near-zero additional marketing cost.

---

## 5. Day-of-week is flat — don't waste budget weighting weekends

Revenue varies by only 5.1% across the entire week (Tuesday $5.09M vs. Thursday $4.84M) — there is no meaningful weekend effect in this dataset.

**Recommendation:** Unless other internal data shows a strong reason to do otherwise, distribute promotional and staffing budget evenly across all seven days rather than over-investing in weekend campaigns, which is the default assumption for most retail businesses but does not hold true here.
