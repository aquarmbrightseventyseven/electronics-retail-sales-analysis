## 1. Laptops and phones dominate revenue despite lower unit volume

| Product | Revenue | Units sold |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Macbook Pro Laptop | $8,037,600 | 4,728 |
| iPhone | $4,794,300 | 6,849 |
| ThinkPad Laptop | $4,129,958.70 | 4,130 |
| Google Phone | $3,319,200 | 5,532 |
| 27in 4K Gaming Monitor | $2,435,098 | 6,244 |

The Macbook Pro Laptop is the single highest-revenue product despite having the **lowest unit volume** of the top 5 — it earns more from fewer, higher-priced sales. By contrast, Apple Airpods Headphones sold **15,661 units** but generated only $2,349,150 in revenue, less than a third of the Macbook Pro's contribution from far fewer units.

**What this means:** The product mix splits into two distinct economics — a small number of high-ticket items (laptops, phones) drive disproportionate revenue, while accessories (headphones, cables) drive volume but contribute comparatively little revenue per unit. Pricing and promotion strategy should treat these as separate categories.

## 2. San Francisco alone generates nearly a quarter of total revenue

| City | Revenue | % of total |
|------|---------|-----------|
| San Francisco (CA) | $8,262,204 | 24.0% |
| Los Angeles (CA) | $5,452,571 | 15.8% |
| New York City (NY) | $4,664,317 | 13.5% |
| Boston (MA) | $3,661,642 | 10.6% |
| Portland (ME) | $449,758 | 1.3% |

San Francisco generates **more than double** the revenue of New York City and nearly **18× more** than Portland, ME — the lowest-performing city.

**What this means:** Revenue is heavily concentrated in California, with San Francisco and Los Angeles together accounting for almost 40% of total revenue. This is both an opportunity (these markets are clearly working) and a risk (over-reliance on two cities). Portland, ME is a clear underperformer and should be reviewed for market viability.

## 3. December is the strongest month — over 2.5× January's revenue

| Month | Revenue | Line items |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| December | $4,613,443 | 24,984 |
| October | $3,736,727 | 20,282 |
| April | $3,390,670 | 18,279 |
| January | $1,822,257 | 9,709 |
| September | $2,097,560 | 11,621 |

December revenue is **153% higher** than January's, with October as the second strongest month. This pattern is consistent with a holiday shopping season driving the year's strongest sales, with a secondary peak in October (likely early holiday shopping or a promotional event).

**What this means:** Inventory and staffing should scale aggressively for Q4, particularly October through December. January and the late-summer months (August, September) are comparatively weak and may benefit from targeted promotions to smooth demand.

## 4. Order activity peaks at midday and early evening — not late night

| Hour | Revenue | Line items |
|------|---------|-------------|
| 12:00 (noon) | $2,316,821 | 12,587 |
| 19:00 (7 PM) | $2,412,939 | 12,905 |
| 11:00 (11 AM) | $2,300,610 | 12,411 |
| 3:00 AM | $145,758 | 831 |
| 4:00 AM | $162,661 | 854 |

There are two clear peaks: **midday (11 AM–1 PM)** and **early evening (6–8 PM)** — consistent with lunch-break and after-work shopping behaviour. Overnight hours (2–5 AM) are by far the quietest, generating less than 7% of the peak hour's revenue.

**What this means:** This is a conventional retail demand pattern, unlike an always-on or overnight service. Marketing pushes (flash sales, notifications) timed around 11 AM–1 PM and 6–8 PM would align with when customers are already most engaged.

## 5. Day-of-week revenue is remarkably flat — no single day dominates

| Day | Revenue |
|-----|---------|
| Tuesday | $5,087,957 |
| Saturday | $4,904,357 |
| Wednesday | $4,988,822 |
| Thursday | $4,839,465 |

The spread between the busiest day (Tuesday) and the quietest (Thursday) is only **5.1%** — essentially flat across the week. This is in contrast to typical retail patterns where weekends usually dominate.

**What this means:** Unlike many retail businesses, this dataset shows no strong weekday/weekend skew. Promotional calendars do not need to be weighted toward weekends — demand is evenly distributed and staffing/inventory planning can treat every day similarly.
